A Tuesday at the hub

It is 7:15 on a Tuesday. The tablet on the kitchen wall is already awake, showing today: two chores, a dentist reminder, and "trash night." Maya walks past, taps her name, and the two things that are hers glow. She taps one done on her way out the door. Her dad taps his name, sees the milk is low because someone flagged it yesterday, and says "Butler, add oat milk" to the speaker on the counter. It lands in this week's cart, waiting for a human to confirm before anything is ordered.

Nobody logged in. Nobody downloaded anything. Nobody was nagged. The household just glanced at the wall and the wall was true. That glance is the product.

Three things Butler does

  • Fair chores everyone can see

    Butler maps the household's chores and shares them out fairly, so the work that usually goes uncounted is visible to everyone. No leaderboard and no nagging, just a clear picture of who does what.

  • A shared hub with no logins

    A tablet on the kitchen wall holds the household: rooms, people, and the chores that attach to both. Walk past, tap your name, and see what is yours. Nobody installs an app and nobody needs a password.

  • Groceries by voice, confirmed by you

    Say "Butler, add oat milk" and it lands in this week's grocery cart. Voice fills the cart; a person in the household always confirms before anything is ordered.
